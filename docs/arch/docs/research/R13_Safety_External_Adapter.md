# R13 — Electrical safety with mains moved into an external adapter

Date: 2026-11-14. Scope: which safety laws/standards apply to the DIN-rail module family when 100–240 V AC is handled only by a separate certified 12 V / ≥30 W adapter. Builds on R01 §3.6 and §5.1 (not repeated).

## 1. Direct answer

**Partly correct.** "The LVD is for the adapter, not the board" — **correct.** "Safety is for the adapter, not the board" — **not correct.**

1. **LVD: correct.** The LVD covers equipment with a rated input or output of 50–1000 V AC / 75–1500 V DC. The modules (9–36 V DC in, ≤ 36 V out) are outside it. The 100–240 V AC adapter is inside it. The Commission's LVD Guide uses this exact case: the battery/low-voltage device is outside the LVD, but "their accompanying power supply unit (e.g. notebooks)" is inside [1, §6].
2. **Radio modules (4G, Wi-Fi/BLE, LoRa): safety still applies to the board.** RED Art. 3.1(a) keeps the LVD safety objectives "but with no voltage limit applying". The Commission's RED Guide (Oct 2026) gives a battery-powered GSM handset as an example [3]. Each radio module needs its own documented safety assessment: electrical, fire, heat, battery/chemical, plus RF exposure. In practice that means a test report to EN IEC 62368-1 (or IEC 61010-2-201). The DoC can be self-declared (Module A).
3. **Non-radio modules (baseboard without radio, fieldbus) sold only B2B: no EU law sets a specific safety requirement at 24 V.** They are outside the LVD and outside the RED. The GPSR covers them only if they are consumer products or likely to reach consumers. They still need CE for EMC, RoHS, WEEE, the Battery Regulation and (from Dec 2027) the CRA. Strict product liability also still applies.
4. **What still applies at 24 V is fire and battery safety, not shock.** Even with the adapter, the board must still deal with:
   - Li-ion charging safeguards (62368-1 Annex M; cells to IEC 62133-2 / IS 16046-2).
   - Power-source (fire) classes. A 30 W adapter is normally **PS2**. A customer's plant 24 V supply makes the board **PS3** (fire enclosure needed) unless the module limits its own input.
   - Enclosure flammability, temperature limits, and short-circuit of the 4 A outputs.
   - EU Battery Regulation: removable battery from 18 Feb 2027, plus labelling.
5. **India and the US force a report anyway.** India: the BIS CRS mark covers the adapter and the cells, and TEC MTCTE's IoT-gateway ER has a safety part. US: OSHA/NRTL listing.

**Bottom line:**
- Moving mains out removes the hardest part of the safety file from the board: reinforced insulation, hipot, mains creepage, surge on the mains port, Class I/II construction. Those are covered by the adapter's own CE/CB/BIS paperwork.
- What remains is a lighter "ES1 product" safety report covering fire, heat, battery and mechanical hazards. It is legally required for the radio modules and commercially required for the rest.

## 3. EU Low Voltage Directive scope

- **Article 1 scope:** the LVD applies to "electrical equipment designed for use with a voltage rating of between 50 and 1 000 V for alternating current and between 75 and 1 500 V for direct current" (quoted in LVD Guide §6 [1]).
- **Ratings, not internal voltages:** "Voltage ratings refer to the voltage of the electrical input or output, not to voltages that may appear inside the equipment." Equipment is in scope if any rated input **or** output lies in the range [1, §6].
- **Battery/low-voltage equipment and its PSU:** "Battery operated equipment outside the voltage rating is obviously outside the scope of the LVD. Nevertheless, any accompanying battery-charger as well as equipment with integrated power supply unit within the voltage ranges of the LVD are in the scope of the LVD. This applies also, in the case of battery operated equipment with supply voltage rating below 50 V AC and 75 V DC, for their accompanying power supply unit (e.g. notebooks)." [1, §6]
- **Consequence for this product:** every module is rated 9–36 V DC in, and its outputs (24 V DO, 3.3/5 V, RS-485, bus 9–36 V DC) are all below 75 V DC, so **the modules themselves are outside the LVD**. The supplied 100–240 V AC adapter is **inside** the LVD (it has a rated input in range) — this matches the notebook example in the Guide.
- **Caveat 1 — radio:** for the radio modules the LVD exclusion does not remove safety; RED Art. 3(1)(a) carries the LVD safety objectives with no voltage limit (§4).
- **Caveat 2 — "supplied with" vs separate product:** if the adapter is placed on the market as part of the product (same box, one DoC), the manufacturer of the kit still has to show LVD conformity for that part; buying in a CE-marked adapter and including its DoC/test report is the usual way (see §8).
- Note: the Commission's LVD page still describes products below the limits as covered by the old General Product Safety *Directive* 2001/95/EC; this has been replaced by the GPSR 2023/988 (§5) [2].
- The LVD Guide in force is the **August 2018** edition (supersedes Nov 2016) [1] — saved locally.

## 4. RED Article 3.1(a) — safety for radio modules

- **Text (as quoted in the Commission's RED Guide, Oct 2026, §3.2 [3]):** "Article 3.1.a RED: the protection of health and safety of persons and of domestic animals and the protection of property, including the objectives with respect to safety requirements set out in Directive 2014/35/EU (LVD), **but with no voltage limit applying**". The Guide adds: "battery-operated radio equipment, such as a GSM handset, is also subject to this essential requirement" and it "covers all health and safety risks arising from the use of equipment, e.g. electrical, mechanical and chemical" [3, p. 52].
- **LVD excluded, its objectives kept:** RED Art. 1(4): "Radio equipment falling within the scope of this Directive shall not be subject to Directive 2014/35/EU (LVD), except as set out in point (a) of Article 3(1)" [3, §10.5]. So the 4G, Wi-Fi/BLE and LoRa modules at 12/24 V DC **do** need a documented safety assessment against the LVD safety objectives (electrical, fire, heat, mechanical, chemical — i.e. the battery), plus RF exposure (also under 3.1(a)).
- **Conformity route for 3.1(a):** Module A (self-declaration, internal production control) is always available (B+C or H optional) — the RED Guide's flow chart shows notified bodies are only needed for 3.2/3.3 when harmonised standards are not fully applied [3, §2.8, p. 36]. The manufacturer may use a non-harmonised standard for 3.1(a) but must "motivate clearly why the radio equipment fulfils applicable essential requirement and why this particular standard can be used" [3, §2.8, p. 40].
- **Which safety standard has presumption under RED?** The Commission's RED summary list of harmonised standards (generated 13.10.2025, saved locally [4]) lists, from CENELEC, only EN 50360, EN 50385, EN 50401, EN 50566 and EN 55035 — **no edition of EN 62368-1, EN 62311 or EN 62479 appears**. The latest RED amendment, Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/2003 (OJ 7.9.2026), concerns radiodetermination and UWB standards [5]. Therefore (as far as verified) **EN IEC 62368-1 gives no formal presumption of conformity under RED**; it is used as the state-of-the-art means to show 3.1(a) compliance, which every EU test lab and notified body accepts in practice. Whether the summary list PDF omits RED-listed IEC-based safety standards by layout — **UNVERIFIED** (the OJ annex of Implementing Decision (EU) 2022/2191 could not be read; EUR-Lex blocks automated access).
- **Stacked modules = separate finished products:** RED Guide §1.7.4.1: RED applies to radio + non-radio product "as a whole only if the radio equipment is incorporated into the non-radio product; and permanently affixed". If it "cannot be easily accessed and readily removed" it is one product; "in the other cases the radio equipment and the non-radio product are considered as separate finished products and only the radio equipment is subject to the RED" [3, p. 17–18]. Snap-together DIN modules sold separately → each radio module is its own RED product; the baseboard alone is not RED equipment. A pre-assembled stack sold as one SKU containing a radio module would be a single RED product (judgement; the Guide notes this section "mainly captures household and similar appliances").
## 5. Non-radio modules outside the LVD — GPSR

- **Scope of GPSR (EU) 2023/988:** Art. 3(1): "'product' means any item, whether or not it is interconnected to other items, supplied or made available, whether for consideration or not, including in the context of providing a service, which is intended for consumers or is likely, under reasonably foreseeable conditions, to be used by consumers even if not intended for them" [6]. Art. 2(1): "Where products are subject to specific safety requirements imposed by Union law, this Regulation applies only to those aspects and risks or categories of risks which are not covered by those requirements" [6]. Applies from 13 Dec 2024 [7].
- **Professional-only products:** supplies to businesses are outside, "unless the products are expressly intended for consumers or likely to be used by consumers under reasonably foreseeable circumstances" [7]. GPSR recital 10 brings in professional products that have "migrated to the consumer market" (quoted via Commission Access2Markets page [8]).
- **What this means for the baseboard (no radio) and the fieldbus module at 9–36 V DC:**
  - Not LVD (below 75 V DC, §3), not RED (no radio).
  - GPSR applies **only if** the module is consumer-facing or likely to reach consumers (e.g. sold on open web shops / marketplaces to hobbyists, home-automation integrators). A DIN-rail industrial controller sold B2B through distributors to panel builders is, on the plain wording, **outside the GPSR**. If sold on Amazon-type channels it is likely inside (judgement).
  - The EMC Directive 2014/30/EU (no safety content), RoHS, WEEE, Battery Regulation and (from 11 Dec 2027) the CRA still apply — so the module is still CE-marked, but **no EU law imposes a mandatory electrical-safety essential requirement** on a B2B-only, non-radio 24 V module. (The LVD Guide/Commission LVD page reasons the same way: below the limits, safety falls to the general product-safety framework [2].)
  - The general civil-liability backstop remains: the revised Product Liability Directive (EU) 2024/2853 makes the manufacturer strictly liable for damage by a defective product regardless of voltage — date of application 9 Dec 2026 is from memory, **UNVERIFIED** in this run.
- **What manufacturers do in practice:** they still run a safety evaluation to EN IEC 62368-1 (or EN 61010-1 + -2-201 for control equipment), because (a) any SKU that can be stacked with a radio module ends up in a RED product needing 3.1(a); (b) TEC MTCTE in India and BIS require a safety report (IS 13252-1 / IS/IEC 62368-1); (c) UL/CSA listing is needed for North America; (d) distributors, system integrators and insurers ask for CB reports; (e) GPSR "migration" risk. Test labs routinely market 62368-1 CB scheme reports for 12/24 V products (judgement; see §6/§7).
- **Recommendation:** treat the non-radio modules exactly like the radio ones: one safety report (CB) covering the whole family, so that EU, India and US paperwork reuse the same tests.

### 6.2 Which standard fits which module
- **IEC 61010-2-201:2024 scope** explicitly includes "programmable logic controller (PLC)", "programmable automation controller (PAC)", "industrial PC", and as components "(auxiliary) stand-alone power supplies", "peripherals such as digital and analogue I/O", "remote-I/O" and "industrial network equipment, embedded or stand-alone (e.g. switches, routers, wireless base station)"; it is for equipment "intended to be used in an industrial environment" [14, §1.1.1]. NOTE 2: equipment conforming to IEC 62368 "[is] considered to be suitable for use with control equipment" [14].
- So **the whole family can be assessed under IEC 61010-1 + IEC 61010-2-201** (baseboard = PLC/remote-IO; fieldbus module = I/O peripheral; 4G/Wi-Fi/LoRa modules = "industrial network equipment … wireless base station"), or the radio modules under IEC 62368-1.
- **Practical recommendation (judgement):**
  - Baseboard and fieldbus module → **IEC 61010-1 + IEC 61010-2-201** (natural fit for 24 V DI/DO field wiring, industrial pollution degree 2, the DIN/PLC market expects it, and UL 61010-2-201 is the US PLC route). If India's TEC/BIS route forces 62368-1, use 62368-1 for both (it is acceptable for an industrial gateway).
  - 4G / Wi-Fi+BLE / LoRa modules → **IEC 62368-1** (ICT; what TEC MTCTE, BIS and most RED labs expect for gateways) — or 61010-2-201 if the whole family goes one way.
  - **Best single choice for one CB report covering the whole family:** IEC 62368-1 if India-first is the priority (BIS/TEC use IS 13252-1 / 62368-1); IEC 61010-2-201 if the product is positioned as a PLC/remote-IO. Mixing is allowed but doubles the lab work for shared parts (battery, bus, enclosure).
- Neither standard is OJ-cited in its latest edition, so presumption of conformity under the LVD rests on the older cited editions; for the 24 V modules the LVD does not apply anyway, so this matters only for the adapter (§8) and as "state of the art" evidence for RED 3.1(a).
## 7. What still applies at low voltage (battery, PS classes, fire, enclosure, IO)

Moving mains out removes the **electric-shock** hazard class from the modules (everything is ES1). It does **not** remove the **fire**, **thermal**, **battery/chemical** and (for 24 V outputs) **overcurrent** hazards — in IEC 62368-1 these are energy-source classes independent of voltage.

### 7.1 Electrical energy source class — ES1 (shock)
- ES1 DC limit: ≤ 60 V at > 2 mA (or > 60 V at ≤ 2 mA), and must not exceed ES2 limits under a single fault of a basic safeguard (IEC 62368-1 5.2.1.1, per IEEE 802.3 PDCC summary table [17]). IEC 61140 SELV: touch voltage ≤ 60 V DC in dry locations [17].
- 9–36 V DC input, 24 V DI/DO, RS-485, CAN, bus: all **ES1/SELV** as long as the supply is itself SELV/ES1 (certified adapter or a SELV/PELV plant 24 V PSU). Consequence: **no basic or reinforced insulation, no hipot, no mains creepage/clearance and no touch-current test on the modules** — only functional spacing. The isolated RS-485 / Ethernet isolation barriers are then functional, not safety, insulation (Ethernet still needs its IEEE 802.3 1500 V isolation for function/EMC, not safety).
- The installation manual must **require** a SELV/PELV (ES1) source ≤ 36 V for the plant-24 V option; otherwise a customer's PSU fault could put mains-derived voltage on the modules. IEC 61010-2-201:2024 added explicit clauses on insulation and wiring of SELV/PELV circuits (6.7.1.101, 6.9.101) [14].

### 7.2 Power source class — PS1/PS2/PS3 (fire)
- Classification by **maximum available power** measured under worst-case load fault and worst-case source fault (UL tech brief on 62368-1 [18]):
  - **PS1:** ≤ 500 W during first 3 s, then ≤ 15 W — not enough to ignite materials.
  - **PS2:** exceeds PS1, ≤ 100 W after 5 s — "ignition possible but the spread of fire can be localized with effective material control or isolation safeguards".
  - **PS3:** exceeds PS2 "or any unclassified source" — needs PS2 measures **plus a fire enclosure** (62368-1 6.4.6) [18][19].
- **A 12 V / 30 W adapter:** class depends on the adapter's maximum *available* power into a fault, not its 30 W rating. Typical 30–36 W adapters with hiccup/OCP limiting deliver ~40–60 W → **PS2**; an adapter rated as LPS/NEC Class 2 is ≤ 100 VA by construction (§10) → PS2 at most. Verify by measurement (UNVERIFIED for the chosen part).
- **Plant 24 V DC supply option — the trap:** a plant 24 V PSU of 5–20 A (120–480 W) is **PS3** at the module input unless the module limits it. The baseboard's 4 × 4 A high-side outputs (≈ 96 W each at 24 V, up to ~384 W total) are PS3 circuits fed from such a supply. Options: (a) accept PS3 → fire enclosure of the baseboard (V-1 or better material, openings rules) and V-1 field-wiring terminals; (b) add a resettable/eFuse input limiter so each module input is ≤ 100 W within 5 s → PS2; (c) restrict in the manual to an LPS/Class 2 source (limits the DO current). A battery cell is also a power source: per UL's 62368-1 FAQ, a fire enclosure "V-1 or better" is required around a lithium cell unless the cell is PS1 (search snippet of UL CRC FAQ [20]; text not fetched — UNVERIFIED).
- **Practical:** most DIN-rail PLC housings already use UL 94 V-0 PC/PA; keep V-0 (or ≥ V-1) for all enclosures and terminal blocks so PS3 is covered regardless of how the customer powers the stack.

### 7.3 Enclosure flammability, temperature, mechanics
- UL 94 V-0/V-1 for enclosure and terminals (above). Glow-wire alternative introduced in 62368-1 ed. 4 for the general flammability requirement (Element summary [21]).
- Temperature limits (accessible surfaces and component/material limits, TS1/TS2) apply at max ambient — DIN modules at 55–70 °C ambient need a heating test with the 4 A outputs fully loaded.
- External wiring connected to PS2/PS3 circuits must meet the cable flammability requirement (62368-1 6.5) — UL FAQ snippet [20] (UNVERIFIED text).

### 7.4 Output short-circuit / overcurrent (24 V DO, bus)
- Single-fault and abnormal-operation tests: short each DO, each bus pin and each connector pin; no fire, no emission of flame/molten metal, no enclosure temperature beyond limits. High-side switches with current limit/thermal shutdown (e.g. 4 A class) plus a per-module input fuse are the normal safeguards.
- Field wiring terminals: 24 V circuits under 61010-2-201 are SELV/PELV; creepage/clearance between 24 V field terminals and other SELV circuits are **functional** only (no safety minimum), but IEC 61010-2-201 OVC II / pollution degree 2 rules apply to any terminal that could be connected to mains — keep the 24 V terminals mechanically distinct (keyed) from any mains-rated terminal in the panel (judgement).
### 7.5 Li-ion cell and charger (every module)
- **IEC 62368-1 ed. 4 (2023) Annex M** — significant change in ed. 4: "revision of battery charging requirements" (IEC foreword, preview saved [19]). Annex M structure: M.2 safety of batteries/cells; M.3 protection circuits; **M.4 additional safeguards for secondary lithium batteries — M.4.2 charging safeguards, M.4.3 fire enclosure, M.4.4 drop test**; M.6 short-circuit safeguards [19]. Normative cell references include IEC 62133-2:2017+AMD1:2021 and IEC 62619:2022 [19].
- M.4.2 wording (quoted in an ENEC decision sheet): "The battery charging circuit shall stop charging when the temperature of the battery exceeds the highest specified charging temperature" and "shall limit the current to the value specified by the battery manufacturer when the battery temperature is lower than the lowest specified charging temperature"; compliance is by measuring charging voltage, current and each cell's temperature under normal, abnormal **and single-fault** conditions (forum quote of the compliance clause) [22]. → NTC on the cell, charger IC with JEITA window, secondary over-voltage protection, and a fault-tolerant design so a single shorted/open NTC or charger FET does not overcharge.
- **Cells:** IEC 62133-2 certified (India: IS 16046-2, see R01 §5.1); transport UN 38.3 test summary. A lithium cell that is not PS1 needs a V-1 or better fire enclosure (UL FAQ snippet [20], UNVERIFIED).
- **Under IEC 61010-1:** battery requirements are thinner (61010-1 cl. 13.2.2 covers battery charging/explosion) — labs commonly add IEC 62368-1 Annex M or IEC 62133-2 evidence anyway (judgement, UNVERIFIED clause number).
- **Outdoor/cabinet temperatures:** at 55–70 °C cabinet ambient the cell will often be above its max charging temperature (typically 45 °C) → charging must be inhibited; design for it.

### 7.6 EU Battery Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 — applies regardless of voltage
- **Portable battery** = sealed, ≤ 5 kg, not designed specifically for industrial uses, not EV/LMT/SLI (Art. 3(1)(9)) [23][24]. A few-Wh Li-ion cell in the module is most likely a **portable battery**; whether it could be an "industrial battery" (designed specifically for industrial use) is an interpretation question (EWRN notes the split "depends on the interpretation of the wording 'specifically designed for industrial uses'") [24] — do not rely on it.
- **Art. 11 removability/replaceability by the end-user applies from 18 Feb 2027** [25]. "Removability … is understood to be possible when the battery can be safely taken out of a device by the end user or an independent professional, with or without the use of tools, avoiding damage to the battery and to the device" [25, §2].
- **Possible derogation, Art. 11(3):** obligations do not apply "where continuity of power supply is necessary and a permanent connection between the product and the respective portable battery is required to ensure the safety of the user and the appliance or, for products that collect and supply data as their main function, for data integrity reasons". Commission examples: "battery-powered devices used in professional weather stations or in laboratories", backup/CMOS batteries, tolling OBE [25, §4]. An IoT data gateway/logger whose backup cell keeps data and time during outages may fit — but the Commission ties it to "main function" being data collection and "a loss of data integrity needs to be at stake" [25]. **Recommendation: design the cell as user-replaceable (holder/connector, commercially available tools) and avoid the argument.**
- **Labelling:** CE marking of batteries from 18 Aug 2024; general label (Annex VI Part A: manufacturer, category, capacity, chemistry…) from 18 Aug 2026 **or 18 months after the implementing act on label formats, whichever later**; QR code from 18 Feb 2027 — secondary sources conflict, **UNVERIFIED** against EUR-Lex [26]. The battery maker usually labels the cell/pack; the product maker must keep the battery's DoC/technical info and supply removal/replacement instructions.
- Reports of a July 2026 Delegated Regulation adding Art. 11 derogations (wearables) — UNVERIFIED, not relevant to DIN modules [27].

## 8. The adapter — what it must carry

### 8.1 EU directives/regulations on the adapter
- **LVD 2014/35/EU** — yes: rated input 100–240 V AC is in range; the LVD Guide names exactly this case ("accompanying power supply unit (e.g. notebooks)") [1, §6]. If the adapter has no radio it is not RED.
- **EMC 2014/30/EU** — yes. **RoHS 2011/65/EU** — yes (not researched here). **WEEE** — yes.
- **Ecodesign — external power supplies:**
  - In force today: **Regulation (EU) 2019/1782**. Definition of EPS, criterion (f): "it is used or intended to be used with electrical and electronic household and office equipment included in Annex I" (quoted from a Commission working draft that reproduces the existing definition [28]; adopted text not fetched — wording UNVERIFIED but consistent with the SIQ summary that 2019/1782 "mainly targeted lower-power supplies for select office and household equipment" [29]). **An adapter supplied for an industrial DIN-rail controller is therefore normally outside 2019/1782.**
  - Successor: **Regulation (EU) 2025/2052 of 13 Oct 2025**, mandatory **14 Dec 2028**, repealing 2019/1782 [29][30]. In the text transmitted to Council (ST 10030/25, May 2025, saved locally [31]) the EPS definition requires that it is "capable of being used with one or more separate **consumer products** that constitute the primary load", output ≤ 60 V DC, "placed on the market with or without the powered consumer product"; 'consumer product' = "intended for consumers or is likely, under reasonably foreseeable conditions, to be used by consumers even if not intended for them" — same test as GPSR [31]. It also brings USB-C/USB-PD interoperability for most EPS, with exemptions e.g. for products "permanently installed in fixed building locations … wall-mounted control panels" (recital 17) [31]. Final OJ wording not read (UNVERIFIED that it matches the draft).
  - **Conclusion:** a 12 V adapter (DIN-rail or wall-plug) shipped with a B2B industrial controller is **likely outside both EPS ecodesign regulations**; a wall-plug type sold through consumer channels could be inside 2025/2052 from Dec 2028 (USB-PD interoperability!). Buy an adapter that already declares 2019/1782 (Level VI) compliance — most catalogue wall-plug adapters do — and the question disappears for the current regulation.
- **Battery Regulation / Common charger (RED Art. 3(4))**: not applicable — the modules are not in the RED common-charger categories (phones, tablets, cameras etc., per Directive (EU) 2022/2380 — judgement).

### 8.2 Safety standard for the adapter
- Wall-plug ICT adapter: **EN IEC 62368-1** (presumption under LVD only via EN 62368-1:2014 citation, §6.1).
- DIN-rail PSU: commonly certified to **IEC 62368-1** and/or **IEC 61010-1 + IEC 61010-2-201** (ed. 3 lists "(auxiliary) stand-alone power supplies" as control-equipment components [14]) and/or **IEC 61558-2-16** (SMPS units; EN 61558-2-16:2009+A1:2013 cited under LVD [9]). Any one is acceptable; for the system safety file, prefer a PSU whose CB report covers the standard you use for the modules (no gaps in SELV/ES1 output and PS classification).
- Output must be **SELV/ES1**, double/reinforced insulated (Class II), ideally **LPS (IEC 62368-1 Annex Q) or NEC Class 2** (≤ 100 VA, §10) — this makes the module inputs PS2 and lets the module report reference the adapter's limited output.

### 8.3 Who is the "manufacturer" of the adapter, and what documents to hold
- If the adapter carries the **supplier's** name and CE marking, the supplier is its manufacturer; the product maker is a distributor/integrator for that item and only needs to keep evidence. If the adapter is **private-labelled** with the product maker's name/trade mark, then "an importer or distributor shall be considered a manufacturer … where he places electrical equipment on the market under his name or trade mark" (LVD Art. 11, LVD Guide §25 [1]) — the product maker must then hold the full LVD/EMC technical file and sign the DoC.
- **Documents to obtain and keep (10 years in the EU):** (1) supplier's EU DoC (LVD + EMC + RoHS [+ Ecodesign if applicable]) — listing the exact model number; (2) **IECEE CB Test Certificate + full CB Test Report** (IEC 62368-1 and/or 61010-1/61558) with national differences for EU (and IN if available); (3) EMC test report (EN 55032/55035 or EN 61000-6-x); (4) photos/label artwork matching the CB report; (5) for India: BIS CRS registration (R-number) in the supplier's name for IS 13252-1 / IS/IEC 62368-1 (§9); (6) for US: UL/ETL listing file number, Class 2/LPS marking (§10); (7) supply-chain change-notification agreement (component changes invalidate the CB report).
- In the **product's** own technical file: reference the adapter by model, its DoC/CB report, and the safety assessment that the adapter output (ES1, PS2) is what the module report assumes. One product DoC for the kit may list LVD (for the adapter) + EMC/RED.
## 9. India

- **Legal basis:** Electronics and Information Technology Goods (Requirements for Compulsory Registration) Order, 2021 (MeitY), amended several times — latest amendments shown by BIS include S.O. 1363(E) of 21 Mar 2025 and S.O. 4362(E) of 25 Sep 2025 (BIS Scheme II page; a search snippet also shows S.O. 1246(E) of 10 Mar 2026 — UNVERIFIED) [32].
- **Power adapter — yes, CRS:** "Power adaptors for IT equipment" is a separate CRS item (No. 16) under **IS 13252 (Part 1)**; "Power adaptors for audio/video" (No. 17, IS 616); "Adapters for household and similar appliances" (No. 42, IS 302-1) [32]. So the 12 V adapter shipped in India must carry the BIS Standard Mark with an R-number registered by its manufacturer.
- **Transition to IS/IEC 62368-1:2023:** MeitY S.O. 4997(E) of 29 Oct 2025 introduced IS/IEC 62368-1:2023 (secondary sources [33]); BIS FAQ of 22 Jul 2026: old standards (IS 13252-1:2010, IS 616:2017) may be used for renewals and new applications only up to **1 Nov 2028**, "the last date of concurrent running"; after that "no new licence shall be granted under IS 13252 (Part 1):2010 or IS 616:2017"; "separate Licences shall continue for products such as Power Adaptors for IT Equipment" [34] (saved locally). → Buy an adapter already registered (or migrated) under IS/IEC 62368-1:2023.
- **Does the gateway/controller itself fall under CRS?** The BIS Scheme II list (64 items) has no category for gateways, routers, modems, industrial controllers, PLCs or IoT devices; nearest are "Automatic data processing machines" (No. 15, IS 13252-1) and smart watches/POS/etc. [32]. A DIN-rail controller or a cellular/LoRa gateway module is **not obviously in any listed CRS category** — but "automatic data processing machine" is interpreted by HS code and a 4G gateway with a CPU could be argued into it. **UNVERIFIED — get a written opinion from BIS/MeitY (or a CRS consultant) per SKU** (same open item as R01 §5.1).
- **Li-ion cells/packs:** item No. 20, sealed secondary cells/batteries, IS 16046 (lithium: IS 16046-2) [32]; registration by the cell/pack maker (R01 [9][10]). Buy registered cells/packs; if the pack is assembled by you (cell + PCM + connector), the pack itself may need its own R-number — UNVERIFIED, check with BIS.
- **QCOs:** the broad "Machinery and Electrical Equipment Safety (Omnibus Technical Regulation) Order, 2024" (which would have applied to electrical equipment generally) was deferred to 1 Sep 2026 and then **revoked by Gazette notification of 16 Jan 2026** (TÜV Rheinland regulatory note [35]; Gazette not read — UNVERIFIED). No QCO specific to PLCs/control equipment was found (UNVERIFIED negative).
- **TEC MTCTE:** the "IoT Gateway" essential requirements include a safety parameter (typically IS 13252-1 / IS/IEC 62368-1 report from a designated lab) — see R01 §5.2; exact test list UNVERIFIED. This is the most likely place where India will force a 62368-1 report for the 4G (and possibly LoRa/Wi-Fi) modules even though no CRS category applies.
- **CEA:** the Central Electricity Authority (Measures relating to Safety and Electric Supply) Regulations govern electrical **installations** (panels, earthing, wiring by licensed contractors), not product certification — relevant to the customer's panel, not to the module approval (from general knowledge; UNVERIFIED in this run).
## 10. US (brief)

- **Legal driver:** no federal certification law for industrial equipment, but OSHA 29 CFR 1910.303(a): electrical equipment is "acceptable only if approved as defined in §1910.399", i.e. in practice listed by an NRTL (UL, CSA, Intertek ETL, TÜV…) [36]; plus NEC/AHJ acceptance of the panel. Customers' panel builders (UL 508A shops) will ask for UL listing or recognition.
- **Standards:** UL 62368-1 (ICT; US adoption of IEC 62368-1, 3rd edition basis currently in US/Canada per CSA guide — UNVERIFIED for 4th-edition dates) for the radio/gateway modules; **UL 61010-1 + UL 61010-2-201** for PLC/remote-IO (UL 61010-2-201 is the US route that replaced UL 508 for PLCs — UNVERIFIED in this run). FCC Part 15/22/24/27 for radios is separate.
- **NEC Class 2 / LPS:**
  - NEC Class 2 sources are limited to ≤ 100 VA at ≤ 30 V (NEC Chapter 9 Tables 11(A)/(B)) and are "inherently energy-limited … intrinsically safe against both electric shock and fire" (secondary summaries [37]); a Class 2 PSU is listed to UL 1310 (Class 2 power units) or UL 5085-3 (Class 2 transformers) — standard names from general knowledge, UNVERIFIED in this run.
  - IEC/UL 62368-1 **LPS** (Annex Q): ≤ 60 V open-circuit, limited current/VA (IEEE 802.3 PDCC summary [17]) — an LPS-rated adapter makes downstream circuits ≤ PS2 by construction.
  - Siemens (UL 508A practice): Class 2 PSUs limit output "to 100VA even in the event of a fault"; "UL-unlisted components may also be used in the 'NEC Class 2 circuit' because it is not necessary for the AHJ to accept the components in this secure control circuit" [38].
- **Does a Class 2 / LPS adapter reduce module testing?** Yes, partly:
  - Shock: nothing extra (already ES1/SELV).
  - Fire: module circuits fed only from the LPS/Class 2 source are PS2 / IEC 61010-1 "limited-energy circuits" → no fire enclosure, simpler material requirements, abnormal tests limited (a 61010-1 test report example shows "9.4 Limited-energy circuit" claimed because the unit is fed by an LPS module [39]).
  - Field wiring can be NEC Class 2 wiring (cheaper, separated from power wiring).
  - **But:** (a) the baseboard 4 A DO channels and the 12 V/30 W supply already limit the stack; a customer plant 24 V supply removes the benefit unless the manual mandates Class 2/LPS or the module adds its own ≤ 100 VA input limiter; (b) the Li-ion cell is its own power source (Annex M still fully applies); (c) the internal module still needs temperature, abnormal-operation and battery tests; (d) a "for use with Class 2 only" restriction must be marked and stated in the instructions.
## 11. Practical conclusion

**What the owner gains by moving mains into the adapter**
1. **No LVD on the modules**: the CE DoC for non-radio modules is EMC (+RoHS/Battery/CRA) only. Radio modules are RED, and their 3.1(a) assessment is a low-voltage one.
2. **ES1/SELV only on the board**: no basic/reinforced insulation, no 3–4 kV hipot, no mains creepage/clearance (R01 §3.6 rules no longer needed), no touch-current, no protective earth, no Class I/II construction, no mains fuse/MOV/surge on a mains port, no OVC II analysis. This also simplifies PCB layout and the enclosure (no tool-only access to hazardous parts).
3. **The mains part of the approval is bought in**: the adapter's DoC + CB report + BIS R-number + UL listing cover shock/mains. One adapter model can be swapped for another certified one without re-testing the modules, as long as the output stays ES1 and its power class is no worse.
4. **Smaller, cheaper safety test plan**: a 62368-1/61010 report for an ES1 device is mostly power-source classification, abnormal/single-fault tests, heating, battery (Annex M) and material checks. That is typically a fraction of a mains-device test programme (cost/lead time UNVERIFIED — get lab quotes).
5. **India**: the module itself likely falls in no CRS category; the CRS burden sits on the adapter and the cell suppliers.

**What remains the product maker's responsibility**
1. RED 3.1(a) safety + RF-exposure assessment and DoC for each radio module, and a host DoC for any pre-assembled stack containing a radio.
2. Fire safety: PS classification with **every allowed supply** (the supplied adapter, plant 24 V, battery). Either limit the module input to ≤ 100 W (eFuse/PTC → PS2) or design for PS3 (V-0/V-1 fire enclosure and terminals). State the allowed supplies in the manual: SELV/PELV, ≤ 36 V, preferably LPS/Class 2.
3. Li-ion safety: certified cells (IEC 62133-2 / IS 16046-2 / UN 38.3), Annex M charging safeguards under single fault, a charge-temperature window that fits cabinet temperatures, and protection against reverse/short.
4. 24 V DO and bus short-circuit/overload behaviour, heating at max ambient, UL 94 materials, markings and instructions.
5. EU Battery Regulation: end-user-replaceable cell from 18 Feb 2027 (or a documented Art. 11(3) derogation), labelling, and the battery DoC.
6. Holding the adapter's compliance evidence (§8.3). If the adapter is private-labelled, the product maker becomes its manufacturer.
7. India TEC MTCTE safety part and US NRTL listing (commercial/legal gate even where EU law is silent).
8. Choosing one standard family for the whole family's CB report (62368-1 or 61010-2-201), so EU, India and US reuse the same tests.

## 12. Open risks
1. **EN IEC 62368-1 harmonisation**: only EN 62368-1:2014 is cited under the LVD (Commission list, 2 Oct 2026). No 62368-1 entry was found in the RED list. Labs will test to the 2023/2024 edition; presumption of conformity is formally weak, so the 3.1(a) justification text in the technical file matters.
2. **RED summary list completeness**: the Commission's RED summary PDF (13.10.2025) may omit IEC-based safety standards. The OJ annex of Implementing Decision (EU) 2022/2191 (as amended up to 2026/2003) was not read (EUR-Lex blocks automated access).
3. **Plant 24 V supply → PS3**: if the module does not limit its input, it needs a fire enclosure. Decide now (eFuse vs V-0 enclosure).
4. **GPSR exposure** if modules are sold through open web shops/marketplaces: they become consumer-reachable, and GPSR obligations (safety assessment, traceability, online-listing info) apply.
5. **Pre-assembled stack SKUs** that contain a radio = one RED product. The whole stack (including the baseboard's 4 A outputs) then falls under 3.1(a).
6. **Battery Regulation Art. 11 (18 Feb 2027)**: the cell must be end-user replaceable unless the Art. 11(3) data-integrity derogation applies. The Commission ties that derogation to "main function" data collection, and our case is not clear-cut.
7. **India CRS category for the gateway**: "automatic data processing machines" or a future IoT category could capture it. Get a written BIS/MeitY opinion. The BIS 62368-1 migration deadline of 1 Nov 2028 applies to adapters/cells sourced now.
8. **EPS ecodesign**: the 2025/2052 final text was not read. If a wall-plug adapter is shipped in consumer-reachable channels, the USB-PD interoperability rules from 14 Dec 2028 could apply.
9. **US**: UL 62368-1 vs UL 61010-2-201 choice, and Class 2 vs 4 A outputs conflict (Class 2 ≤ 100 VA cannot feed 4 × 4 A).
10. Unverified technical numbers (PS3 fire-enclosure material, UL FAQ battery V-1 rule, IEC 61010-1 limited-energy limits): confirm with the chosen lab against the standard text.
